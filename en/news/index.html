5 November 2026

The Madrid Summit boosts Ibero-American cooperation on artificial intelligence

Ibero-American governments agreed in Madrid on an intergovernmental AI cooperation mechanism built on three pillars: data, models in our languages and computing capacity. At the Alliance we welcome it, and we bring the piece that connects those foundations with businesses: adoption.

Ministers meeting in Madrid on 3 November agreed, and the 30th Ibero-American Summit of Heads of State and Government endorsed on 4 and 5 November, an intergovernmental mechanism for cooperation on artificial intelligence, which States may join on a voluntary basis.

Its three pillars are a federated Ibero-American data space, in which each country decides what it shares and on what terms; support for open, documented and auditable models in Spanish, Portuguese and Indigenous languages, building on those that already exist; and a map of the region's computing capacity to allocate computing hours to public-interest projects that can be reused in other countries.

This is good news for both sides of the Atlantic. The Ibero-American region, home to nearly 700 million people, with more than 850 million speakers of Spanish and Portuguese worldwide, needs data, models and computing that work for it. But infrastructure only creates value when it reaches businesses. That is what our Alliance brings, as a public-private initiative that complements the governments' mechanism: taking AI to startups and SMEs through replicable use cases, training and hands-on support.

We also call for three things to make the new framework work: that federated data rest on interoperability and portability; that computing hours also reach startups and SMEs, under neutral criteria that allow public infrastructure and commercial clouds to be combined; and that open models come with real use cases built by the startup ecosystem.

Sources: La Moncloa (3 Nov 2026) · Infobae (5 Nov 2026, in Spanish) · La Ecuación Digital (in Spanish)

5 November 2026

Rights and innovation go hand in hand: the Madrid Declaration

The declaration adopted at the Ibero-American Summit states that protecting rights and fostering innovation are complementary. It is the idea we have championed since the Alliance began.

The Madrid Declaration, adopted at the 30th Ibero-American Summit, states that protecting rights and fostering innovation are complementary. The fact that governments on both sides of the Atlantic have endorsed it strengthens an approach we share: clear, proportionate rules that protect people without holding back innovation or competitiveness.

To make this principle real in practice, we propose interoperable frameworks between Ibero-America and Europe, testing environments that startups can access, technological neutrality and freedom to choose a provider. This is the path to open strategic autonomy, built on our own capabilities, competition and interoperability, not on closing markets.

Sources: La Moncloa (3 Nov 2026) · Infobae (5 Nov 2026, in Spanish)
